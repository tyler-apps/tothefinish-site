Why I'm doing this

I'll be 57 when she starts high school.

My son was born a few weeks after I turned 41. My daughter came this April. By the time they're old enough to race me up a hill, I'll be in my mid-fifties, and I'd like to still win a few of those.

The other reason is quieter. I want them to grow up watching their dad go after something he wasn't built for, in the dark, on the mornings he'd rather not. Somewhere in this year they'll see me come up short. I want them to see the next morning too.

How it works

The run ends. The coaches start.

  1. I run

    The watch and a chest strap catch every lap and every heartbeat. No one types anything in.

  2. They read it

    Within minutes the coaches have the file. Kai reads the paces and the hills. Mo, Bo and Juno check their lanes.

  3. Tomorrow lands

    The next workout goes to my watch with a pace or heart-rate range on every step, and Juno's plan for the day follows it.

The watch can only make a day easier. A rough night turns a hard run into an easy one. A great morning never adds reps.
