Mo · Recovery

My best trait, on the wrong day.

I like to push. On a hard day that's the whole point. On an easy day it costs me. On November 11 I ran six easy miles and spent 45 minutes of them over my easy heart rate. Mo saw every one of those minutes.

This is the fight I'm in all year: getting slower on the easy days so I can get faster on the hard ones. The best distance runners in the world keep at least 80% of their running easy. Here's how I'm doing. If you run your easy days too hard too, you'll know this chart.

How easy were the easy days?

Running time by lap heart rate: easy is my zone 2 or below (under 121), hard is threshold and up (152+), steady is everything between. Hills on Mission Trails push some easy miles into steady.

Mo

No run too far past the last long one

A study of 5,205 runners on Garmin watches found the injury risk wasn't in the weekly miles. It was in one run more than 10% longer than the longest of the month before. That run raised overuse injuries 1.64 times. So Mo watches each run, not the week.

Each dot is a run, in minutes (trail miles are slower, so time is the fair measure). The line is 10% past the longest run of the 30 days before it.

Mo

Bo · Strength

The bar waits for the miles.

Bo would have me in the garage every day. He doesn't get that. The run comes first, and the bar comes hours later: short, heavy sets of three, never a grind, then a few jumps. In the studies, heavy lifting plus jumps made runners faster than any other kind of strength work, and light circuits did nothing. In marathoners my age it made marathon pace about 6% cheaper to hold. Three lifting days a week now, two by summer.

Juno · Fuel

Friday decides Thursday's dinner.

A hard run starts the night before, with a sweet potato. Then a banana with honey in the dark before I head out, rice at lunch after. An easy day stays light: eggs, berries, greens. Any run past an hour, I'm drinking about 60 grams of carbs an hour while I go. Juno builds every day from what the run is about to ask for.

Grams of carbs a day from my meal plan, the next seven days. Orange days have a hard or long run.

Rook · How the coaching works

The run ends. The coaches start.

No one types my workouts in. I stop my watch, it syncs, and within minutes the coaches have the file: Kai reads the paces and the climbs, Mo the heart rate on the hills, Bo and Juno what it means for tomorrow. By the time I'm in the shower, tomorrow's workout is on my wrist with a range on every step. Here's the count so far.

1

Record

Watch and chest strap: every lap, every heartbeat, every climb.

2

Sync

Checked every 10 minutes. A new run is pulled in as soon as it lands.

3

Read

Kai reads the run: paces, heart rate, the ground. Bo, Juno and Mo check their lanes.

4

Decide

Targets, zones and the next sessions move by the research's rules, and each change is logged.

5

Send

The new workouts go to the watch, with the pace or heart-rate range on every step.

Decisions, logged

Each one is a dated line in the coaches' logs: a pace target that moved, a run rewritten, a zone reset, a plan change. Orange are Kai's own automatic calls from the data. White are head-coach decisions across the plan.

My heart-rate zones, set by Kai from my own race

Threshold comes from my last race, minute 10 to the finish. Kai re-checks them after every sync and moves them only on a race or a field test. Next, the strap's beat-to-beat data will measure exactly where easy ends.

How the coaches decide

The research sets the rules. The watch can only slow me down.

  1. Speed first. A 5K in February, a 10K in May, a half in September, then Chicago. Miles climb to about 90 a week in August.
  2. Hills every week. Short hill sprints on Mondays, longer hill repeats on hard days, most easy miles on Mission Trails dirt.
  3. The watch only makes a day easier. A rough night or a low recovery week turns a hard run into an easy one. A great morning never adds reps.
  4. Heavy lifting, short sessions. Legs on the hard-run days, hours after the run. Top sets of three, never a grind, with jumps.
  5. Fuel the work. Light on easy days, carbs around the hard ones, and 60 grams an hour in any run past an hour.
