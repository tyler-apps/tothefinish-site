The dad behind the watch

I’m Tyler.
I have to run it.

I started at 258 lb, I’ve got a two-year-old and a baby, and I hired five AI coaches to get me under three hours in the marathon. They argue over every run.

Why Chicago

My son was born a few weeks after I turned 41. My daughter came this April. I’ll be 57 when she starts high school. I’d like to still win a few races up a hill by then.

I want them to see me go after something I might miss, and see what I do the next morning.

I’ve run faster before

In June 2024 I ran a half marathon in 1:32:31. That is still my best. When I first asked my watch about a marathon on October 19, 2026, it estimated 4:39:49. My goal for Chicago on October 10, 2027 is under three hours. I don’t know yet whether I’ll get there.

What’s real, and what’s drawn

The training is mine. The watch records my runs, and the coaches read that data to help plan the next day. The five coaches are AI characters with animated faces and AI voices. They can change a plan. They can’t do the run or feel my pain.

The episodes turn those weeks into scenes. The site shows the estimates beside the goals and race results, so you can check how the chase is going. This is my personal coaching setup, not medical or nutrition advice.
