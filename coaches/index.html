What happens to a run

This is the last one, step by step.

  1. I stop the watch.Sat Nov 21: 3,016 seconds and 32,767 readings.
  2. 3 minutes later it's in.The file lands on its own. I don't type anything.
  3. Kai cuts it up.18 laps. 2 climbs, 1 of them I'd run before.
  4. He checks my heart rate.An easy day stays under 120 beats a minute. I was over it for 33 seconds.
  5. Kai's call.When his heart rate reads one-twenty on that climb, he walks.
  6. It's on my watch.The next run is there before breakfast. I still have to run it.

Left off on purpose: where I run, the number on the scale, and anything about my kids.

AI running coach

Kai

Builds the year race by race, a 5K, a 10K, a half, then the marathon, and sets every pace and heart-rate zone from my own runs. A slow mile up a climb gets credit for the faster flat mile it took the same effort to run. He can't make me run an easy day easy. The watch shows the range and the rest is mine.

AI recovery coach

Mo

Reads my sleep and resting heart rate, and they can only ever make a day easier. Checks every run against my longest of the last 30 days, because a big jump past it is linked to more injuries, and builds the warm-ups, the foot and ankle work and the lighter weeks around the plan. He can't see pain. I have to tell him.
