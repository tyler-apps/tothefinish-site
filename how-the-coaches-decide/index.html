The coaches

How the coaches decide.

Five AI coaches plan my training, and they argue about it. They have one runner, and it's me. I started at 258 lb and I'm training for a sub-3 marathon at Chicago on October 10, 2027.

Who does what

Kai plans every run from my Garmin data. He's usually facing east before the sun is up, already talking about the hill. His one law: easy days stay easy, even when I don't want them to.

Bo fits short, heavy lifts around the running. He laughs louder than anyone and leaves chalk on everything, and he'll cut his own session short to protect a long run.

Juno plans every meal around the training. She's quick and she teases, and she has no patience at all for me skipping breakfast before a hard run.

Mo watches my recovery. He carries a roll of tape and a lacrosse ball, asks one precise question, and never clears pain to train.

Rook is the head coach. When the others want different things from the same week, he says one line, and that's the call.

When the coaches disagree

Rook settles every clash in the same order. What I say and any pain I report come first, then the race in Chicago, then my weight, then strength. So when Bo wants heavy legs the day before a hard run, the run wins, and Bo takes it better than you'd think.

The plan comes from the research. Every change is written down with the rule behind it. The coaches are AI characters, and this is my personal setup, not medical or nutrition advice.

What a number can’t tell them

A slower pace can mean a hill, a walk or a tired runner. They have to read the pace, cadence and terrain together. They still need me to report pain. A watch estimate is evidence to check, never a race result or a promise that I’m ready.

What the coaches can read from my watch

During a run

Heart rate, pace, distance, elevation, laps, cadence, stride length, ground contact time and balance, vertical movement, power and breathing estimates. Beat intervals are available when the recording supports them.

Between runs

Sleep duration, overnight heart-rate variability, resting heart rate and training history. The coaches compare trends instead of treating each number as a verdict.

Beyond the watch

My strength log, food records, race calendar and what I report. The watch doesn’t know everything I ate or whether something hurts.

Available fields vary by recording and sensor. Missing data stays missing. Power, sleep stages and race predictions are not automatic permission to train harder.

What happens automatically
  1. Watch → Garmin ConnectMy watch uploads when it syncs.
  2. New data → coaching checksA local job checks for changes every 10 minutes, then imports the records and runs the rules.
  3. Checks → my dayThe run, recovery and weekly-volume checks rebuild the plan and record changes.
  4. Approved plan → GarminThe workout and heart-rate zones sync back. The next recording starts the loop again.

This chain runs on my own setup. Research updates enter through dated reviews; pain reports and changes to my food rules still need my input. My laptop and Garmin connection need to be available.

What runs automatically?

My laptop checks for new Garmin records every 10 minutes. When new data arrives, scripts run the coaching checks, rebuild my day and send approved workout changes and heart-rate zones back to Garmin. My laptop and Garmin connection need to be available. I still report pain and decide changes to my food rules.

Five fresh AI conversations don’t run on every sync.

How does research change the plan?

When we review research, we write down what it changes in my plan, where it came from and what it can’t tell us. The scheduled checks then apply those rules to my data. On Nov 21, a review removed the rule that let one low HRV reading change several training days. Changing a rule doesn’t tell me whether I’ll run faster. I need the races to find that out.
